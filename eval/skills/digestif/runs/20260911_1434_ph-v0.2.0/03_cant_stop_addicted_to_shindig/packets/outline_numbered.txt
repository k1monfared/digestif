L007:         - 2 Four-dice pairing flattens the probability distribution versus two dice. (¶7, ¶8, ¶9)
L008:             - 2.1 Evidence: Two-dice baseline: 7 hits 16.67%, six times more likely than 2. (¶7)
L009:             - 2.2 Evidence: Four dice yield exactly three possible pairings per roll. (¶9)

L017:         - 4 Stopping rule weighs expected gain against bust-weighted unsaved progress. (¶17, ¶18, ¶19)
L018:             - 4.1 Evidence: Roll on {6,7,8} with five steps banked; stop on {2,3,12} with two. (¶17)
L019:             - 4.2 Nuance: Only 39.8% of {6,7,8} rolls are clean; {2,3,12} just 2.3%. (¶18)
L020:             - 4.3 Concession: Continuing current columns usually beats switching, but author hedges: switching unanalyzed quantitatively. (¶19)
L021:         - 5 3.6M-game simulations crown probabilistic, consistent strategies over fast ones. (¶20, ¶22)
L022:             - 5.1 Evidence: 38 strategy families tested across single-player and head-to-head simulations. (¶20)

L024:             - 5.3 Evidence: FiftyPercentSurvival wins 69.84% by stopping below 50% cumulative survival. (¶22)
L025:             - 5.4 Nuance: Consistency beats raw speed; pure greedy loses nearly everything. (¶23, ¶25)
L026:             - 5.5 Evidence: First player wins 55.59%, an 11.18% edge exceeding chess and Go. (¶24)
L027:         - 6 Board is nearly balanced singly; forced moves compensate combination imbalance. (¶26, ¶27, ¶28)
