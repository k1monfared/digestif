L002:     Summary: Simulated cultural markets show patronage and feedback manufacture canons: quality filters but capital decides. (¶1, ¶2, ¶5, ¶45, ¶64)
L003:         - 1 Canon question: we remember under 20 of thousands; patrons, not merit, may have chosen. (¶2, ¶3)
L004:             - 1.1 Evidence: Vienna had 200 to 500 active composers; IMSLP catalogs over 3,400 survivors. (¶3)
L005:             - 1.2 History cannot rerun; Haydn without Esterhazy patronage is empirically unanswerable. (¶4)
L006:         - 2 Project tests three competing stories of canon formation. (¶6, ¶7)
L007:             - 2.1 Story 1 Cream Rises: quality wins over time; replayed history yields same canon. (¶8)
L008:             - 2.2 Story 2 Money Writes History: early exposure becomes familiarity, then canon; quality nearly irrelevant. (¶9)
L009:             - 2.3 Story 3 Floors and Ceilings: extremes decided by quality, middle by luck and money. (¶10)
L010:             - 2.4 Example: Job-market analogy: top and bottom rank easily; middle hires depend on luck. (¶11)
L011:         - 3 Model combines three documented mechanisms to quantify their joint canon effect. (¶12, ¶13)
L012:             - 3.1 Evidence: Mere exposure: liking grows with familiarity; Zajonc 1968, Bornstein 1989 r equals 0.26. (¶14) [reported]
L013:             - 3.2 Nuance: Exposure advantage contaminates sincere judgment; money set the exposure. (¶15)
L014:             - 3.3 Evidence: Social influence snowballs popularity; Salganik MusicLab 2006 ranked same song 1st or 40th. (¶16) [reported]
L015:             - 3.4 Evidence: Cumulative advantage magnifies small starts; Merton Matthew Effect compounds court posts. (¶17) [reported]
L016:             - 3.5 Open question: what happens when all three interact over generations. (¶18)
L017:         - 4 Simulation uses producers, consumers, gatekeepers with independent quality and capital scores. (¶19, ¶20)
L018:             - 4.1 Nuance: Quality and capital are uncorrelated; wealth does not make talent. (¶20)
L019:             - 4.2 Capital buys exposure; consumers add quality, exposure, social signals; success feeds exposure. (¶21)
L020:             - 4.3 Evidence: Thousands of runs; 1,000 producers, 10,000 consumers; 360 plus per condition with power analysis. (¶22)
L021:         - 5 Results favor capital-plus-feedback over pure quality across experiments. (¶23)
L022:             - 5.1 Evidence: Visible social influence significantly weakens quality-success link, p below 0.0001. (¶24, ¶25)
L023:             - 5.2 Charts push us from independent judgment toward optimizing a popularity proxy. (¶26)

L026:             - 5.5 Without Esterhazy hire, Haydn likely joins forgotten hundreds despite excellence. (¶31)
