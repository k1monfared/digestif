L001: - The Case for Complete Government Transparency (source: 02_case_for_transparent_government/source.txt)
L002:     Summary: Citizens are governments' CEO-principals, so every decision should be fully documented and traceable, with objections answered and precedents proving feasibility. (¶1, ¶3, ¶4, ¶9, ¶18, ¶24, ¶60, ¶64)
L003:         - 1 A manager hiding a million-dollar decision would be fired, yet governments do the same. (¶1, ¶2)
L004:             - 1.1 Example: Manager refused to explain a million-dollar decision, citing lost leverage. (¶1)
L005:             - 1.2 An owner would sue, force disclosure, and fire that manager. (¶2)
L006:             - 1.3 Governments refuse with classified, security, operations, and speed excuses. (¶2)
L007:         - 2 Every government decision should be fully documented and publicly available. (¶3)
L008:             - 2.1 Document data used, considerations made, and who was involved. (¶3)
L009:             - 2.2 Document alternatives considered, arguments for and against, implementation steps. (¶3)
L010:             - 2.3 Document evaluation, course-correction plans, and reconsideration terms. (¶3)
L011:             - 2.4 Competent boards and CEOs already demand this basic accountability. (¶4)
L012:             - 2.5 It sounds radical only because the opposite was normalized. (¶4)
L013:         - 3 In democracy citizens are principals; officials are their employee-agents. (¶4)
L014:             - 3.1 Every servant from licensing clerk to president works for you. (¶4)
L015:             - 3.2 CEOs need not read everything, but reviewability shapes behavior. (¶5)
L016:             - 3.3 A company letting managers classify emails would go bankrupt within a year. (¶5)
L017:             - 3.4 Nuance: Availability matters, not consumption: trace decisions affecting your life. (¶6)
L018:         - 4 Transparency flows from government to citizens, never the reverse. (¶7)
L019:             - 4.1 Decisions visible, processes traceable, officials accountable to citizens. (¶7)
L020:             - 4.2 Not tax returns, medical records, or citizens surveilling each other. (¶7)
L021:             - 4.3 CEOs see work product, not employees' medical records. (¶7)
L022:             - 4.4 Example: Publish $X/$Y/$Z/$W spending, not John Smith's $47,000 taxes. (¶8)
L023:             - 4.5 Aggregation, anonymization, and differential privacy make separation technically straightforward. (¶8)
L024:         - 5 Pieces of this system already exist and prove it works. (¶9)
