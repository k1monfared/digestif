L002:     Summary: Feature matrix contrasts cognitive and affective empathy, sympathy, and compassion across five profiles from psychopath to integrated ideal. (¶1, ¶2, ¶28)
L003:         - 1 Theme: Cold-control block: rows present in both dark profiles, plus the psychopath-only control row. (¶2, ¶3)
L004:             - 1.1 Accurate modeling: cognitive ✓, affective ~, sympathy ~, compassion ✓; psychopath ✓, sociopath ~, empath ~, altruist ✓, integrated ✓. (¶4)

L006:             - 1.3 Manipulators row: cognitive ✓ only; psychopath ✓, sociopath ~, all others blank. (¶6)
L007:             - 1.4 Controlled/instrumental, not reactive/impulsive: cognitive ✓, sympathy ~, compassion ✓; psychopath ✓, altruist ✓, integrated ✓; affective, sociopath, empath blank. (¶7)

L021:         - 4 Theme: Notes interpret the matrix rows and limit its scope to ideal types. (¶19, ¶29)
L022:             - 4.1 Nuance: Sympathy is '~' on modeling minds: one can pity while badly misreading; understanding helps but isn't constitutive, unlike cognitive empathy. (¶19)
L023:             - 4.2 Nuance: Affective empathy's care is '~': contagion can happen involuntarily, without real concern, and simply make one want to get away. (¶20)
L024:             - 4.3 Evidence: 'Manipulators' row is the sharpest diagnostic and essentially the psychopath's signature: cognitive empathy alone is compatible with cruelty. (¶21)
L025:             - 4.4 Nuance: Sustainability row reflects the Bloom/Singer argument (Paul Bloom's Against Empathy; Tania Singer's research), a position not a consensus; critics warn of abstract, paternalistic compassion. (¶22) [reported]
L026:             - 4.5 Boundary maintenance is the structural difference: partially merging with the other's state drives both motivational power and burnout/bias problems. (¶23)
L027:             - 4.6 Dark-column reading: psychopath is the cognitive column standing alone; sociopath is affect plus sympathy clamped to a narrow in-group, stripped of regulation. (¶24)
L028:             - 4.7 Nuance: Asymmetry: almost everything the psychopath has, the sociopath shares in degraded form; the psychopath's one distinguishing possession is cold instrumental control. (¶24)
L029:             - 4.8 Nuance: Etiology deliberately left out: psychopathy is often theorized as innate, sociopathy as environmentally acquired; causes don't describe the response's shape. (¶25)
L030:             - 4.9 Nuance: Clinical caveat: the two profiles aren't formal diagnoses; both fall under Antisocial Personality Disorder; the split is a contested, largely theoretical convention. (¶26)
L031:             - 4.10 Nuance: Terminology varies: some authors use 'empathic concern' roughly where the table says compassion; this table uses the neutral research sense of sympathy. (¶27)
L032:             - 4.11 Empath profile: affective column maxed and unregulated, failing boundary, scope, and scalability; inherits the sociopath's distortions, so not the moral ideal. (¶28)
L033:             - 4.12 Altruist profile: compassion column embodied, matching the research 'Light Triad' (Kaufman et al., 2019). (¶28)
L034:             - 4.13 Integrated ideal: the only column scoring well across every block, including the 'present in neither' rows; true opposite of both dark profiles. (¶28)
L035:             - 4.14 Nuance: Kantianism row is essentially the inverse of the manipulators row; Humanism overlaps the caring and beyond-the-in-group rows. (¶29)
L036:             - 4.15 Nuance: Only Faith in Humanity adds a genuinely new dimension, an attitude rather than an empathy component; both dark profiles score blank there. (¶29)
