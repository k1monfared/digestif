L001: - What Do We Value When We Value Consistency, point graph (source: source.txt)
L002:     Summary: Consistency of behavior is a bad proxy: what matters is whether the anchor moves on evidence or cost, which only a prior prediction can reveal. (¶1, ¶2, ¶4, ¶49)
L003:         - 1 Theme: The post asks what consistency actually means, since it carries moral weight but no structure. (¶2)
L004:             - 1.1 The author keeps reaching for consistency, a word vaguer than he would like, attaching moral value to it: sometimes a compliment, sometimes an accusation. (¶2)
L005:             - 1.2 Example: Before the 1979 revolution in Iran, someone the author's mom knew wore mini skirts; right after it she switched to chador and niqab. (¶3)
L006:             - 1.3 Example: Others in the same circle dressed modestly before the revolution and modestly after, changing only slightly how much they covered. (¶3)
L007:             - 1.4 The instinct reads the swing as a moral failing and the steadiness as integrity. (¶4)
L008:                 - 1.4.1 A more cynical observer might call the first woman cunning, quick to adapt, and the others slow to catch up. (¶4)
L009:             - 1.5 Nuance: Even those who judge her harshly tend to agree that updating beliefs on certain topics is a virtue and that nobody should be stoneheaded. (¶4)
L010:             - 1.6 So the swing itself cannot be what we are judging, and plain behavioral consistency is not carrying the moral weight the author assumed. (¶4)
L011:             - 1.7 The post is an attempt to find the something underneath consistency. (¶4)
L012:         - 2 Theme: A small model puts numbers on the clothing story to find what consistency is really measuring. (¶5)
L013:             - 2.1 Clothing openness is measured from -1 (fully closed) to 1 (fully open), with society's average at -0.5 closed and 0.5 open. (¶5)
L014:             - 2.2 The modest woman is observed at -0.1 in the closed state and 0.1 in the open state: a slight change, but not zero. (¶5)
L015:             - 2.3 Hypothesis: her behavior blends her own fixed point and the social reference, Behavior = w x Reference + (1 - w) x Anchor. (¶6, ¶7)
L016:             - 2.4 The Anchor is what she would do if the reference had no pull; w, the Responsiveness, is how much weight the reference actually carries. (¶8)
L017:             - 2.5 Evidence: Two observations give two equations: -0.5w + (1-w)a = -0.1 and 0.5w + (1-w)a = 0.1. (¶9)
L018:             - 2.6 Evidence: Adding them gives 2(1-w)a = 0, so the anchor is a = 0; subtracting gives w = 0.2. (¶10)
L019:             - 2.7 Her behavior is exactly 0.2 x Reference both times: a fixed anchor at neutral, yielding a fixed twenty percent of the distance toward society. (¶10)
L020:             - 2.8 Her output moved, -0.1 then 0.1, no fixed value; her rule never did: same anchor, same responsiveness, both times. (¶11)
L021:             - 2.9 Nuance: An unusually stable person, conceding a fixed fraction of ground under pressure. (¶11)
L022:             - 2.10 The chameleon woman is at 0.9 when society is 0.5, and at -0.9 when society is -0.5. (¶12)
L023:             - 2.11 Evidence: Her anchor comes out to 0 again, and her responsiveness comes out to 1.8: she does not just follow the reference, she amplifies it. (¶13, ¶14)
L024:             - 2.12 Her rule is exactly as consistent as the modest woman's: one anchor, one responsiveness, both times. (¶14)
L025:             - 2.13 Nuance: Judging only her output would call her a fashion-following chameleon, and that output judgment is fair. (¶14)
L026:             - 2.14 But the inference that comes with it, that she has no fixed point and stands for nothing, is wrong. (¶14)
L027:             - 2.15 The difference between the two women is what the rule says: concede a fifth of the distance to society, or overshoot it by eighty percent. (¶14)
L028:             - 2.16 Consistency can be asked of output, policy, and anchor independently: did the behavior, the rule, or the fixed point itself stay fixed? (¶15)
L029:             - 2.17 The two women fail the first in opposite amounts, one barely and one spectacularly, and pass the second and third completely. (¶15)
L030:             - 2.18 Nuance: An observer watching only output would see a consistency gap between them that does not exist at either deeper layer. (¶15)
L031:             - 2.19 The third layer is where the real action is: policy can stay fixed while the anchor drifts underneath, invisibly, in undramatic steps. (¶16)
L032:             - 2.20 Nuance: No single moment of that drift feels like a change of mind, and that invisible drift is where the moral weight lives. (¶16)
L033:             - 2.21 Nuance: A deliberate omission: cases where the anchor is closer to or farther from the reference, unexplored for lack of meaningful examples to study or interpret. (¶50)
L034:             - 2.22 Nuance: An economist might call the anchor an endogenous variable and the reference an exogenous one; the author prefers words he can relate to. (¶51)
L035:             - 2.23 The model is the simplest the author could think of: a linear convex combination whose coefficient need not lie between 0 and 1. (¶51)
L036:         - 3 The contrarian next door runs the same model with the sign flipped and the magnitude doubled. (¶17, ¶20)
L037:             - 3.1 A third woman is observed at 0.2 when the average is -0.5, and at -0.2 when the average is 0.5. (¶17)
L038:             - 3.2 Evidence: Her anchor comes out to 0 again, and her responsiveness comes out to -0.4. (¶18, ¶19)
L039:             - 3.3 She has a fixed neutral point and a fixed reaction coefficient, pushing away from the reference instead of toward it. (¶20)
L040:             - 3.4 We usually file contrarians under independent-minded, but the arithmetic disagrees. (¶21)
L041:             - 3.5 She is more dependent on the social reference than the modest woman in absolute terms, because reliably doing the opposite requires tracking it closely. (¶21)
L042:             - 3.6 Contrarianism is dependency with a minus sign, not independence. (¶21)
L043:         - 4 Theme: The values of w carve out a named family of postures, all instances of the same equation. (¶22)
L044:             - 4.1 Above 1 is the Amplifier, overshooting the reference in whichever direction it moves: the chameleon from the story. (¶23)
L045:             - 4.2 At exactly 1 is the Absolute conformist: the anchor drops out of the formula. (¶23)
L046:                 - 4.2.1 Behavior equals the reference exactly, always, with no fixed point to reconstruct a belief from. (¶23)
L047:             - 4.3 Between 0 and 1 is the Partial conformist: a real anchor and a real pull toward the reference, the modest woman. (¶23)
L048:             - 4.4 At 0 is the Independent: behavior ignores the reference entirely, the only posture requiring no information about it. (¶23)
L049:             - 4.5 Between -1 and 0 is the Partial contrarian: a real anchor and a real push away from the reference, the contrarian. (¶23)
L050:             - 4.6 At -1 is the Absolute contrarian: maximal dependence inverted, needing to track the reference more precisely than the conformist to oppose it reliably. (¶23)
L051:             - 4.7 The independent is the only posture that does not need to know what the reference is doing; every other is a reference-tracking system. (¶24)
L052:             - 4.8 Nuance: In that narrow sense the absolute conformist and the absolute contrarian sit closer to each other than either sits to the independent. (¶24)
L053:             - 4.9 Nuance: Below -1 sits the mirror image of the chameleon, a contrarian who overshoots: an extremist rebel? (¶24)
L054:         - 5 Theme: The model is morally neutral until you ask what kind of reference is in play. (¶25)
L055:             - 5.1 A normative reference, such as peer pressure, fashion, or social approval, is something your anchor is supposed to resist. (¶26)
L056:             - 5.2 High responsiveness to a normative reference is suspect by default. (¶26)
L057:             - 5.3 A structural reference, such as a deadline, a budget, a body, or a risk exposure, is something your anchor is supposed to track. (¶27)
L058:             - 5.4 Zero responsiveness to a structural reference is not principle; it is malfunction. (¶27)
L059:             - 5.5 Nuance: In an ideal world, the author says, |w| should generally be small and everyone should have an educated Anchor. (¶28)
L060:                 - 5.5.1 Nuance: But this is not an ideal world, and not everyone can get educated about everything. (¶28)
L061:             - 5.6 Example: A common rule of thumb sets equity allocation at roughly 110 minus your age. (¶29)
L062:                 - 5.6.1 Example: That puts about 85% in stocks at 25 and about 50% at 60, tapering further as retirement nears. (¶29)
L063:             - 5.7 Someone following it, aggressive at 25 and cautious at 60, is not inconsistent: their reference is the years until the money is needed. (¶29)
L064:             - 5.8 The right exposure to market swings genuinely is a function of that horizon. (¶29)
L065:                 - 5.8.1 A 25-year-old can ride out a crash over decades; a retiree drawing down savings cannot. (¶29)
L066:             - 5.9 Here, holding behavior fixed across a life would be the failure, not the virtue. (¶29)
L067:             - 5.10 Example: An investor who stays at 85% equities at 65 because he has always been aggressive refuses to let a correctly varying function vary. (¶29)
L068:             - 5.11 The clothing conformist and the lifecycle investor look superficially alike, behavior moving as a reference moves, but morally they are opposites. (¶30)
L069:             - 5.12 Holding output fixed against a moving reference reads as integrity in the clothing case and as ossification in the investing case. (¶30)
L070:             - 5.13 Same form, opposite verdict: the verdict depends entirely on which kind of reference is being held fixed against. (¶30)
L071:         - 6 Theme: The harder question is why the anchor moves, because that is where the moral weight actually lives. (¶31)
L072:             - 6.1 The world's facts can change, or your grasp of them can, giving you new evidence. (¶32)
L073:             - 6.2 Or the cost of expressing a given position can change, with no new evidence at all. (¶32)
L074:             - 6.3 Crossing those forces with whether the anchor actually moved gives four cells. (¶32, ¶33)
L075:             - 6.4 Rational revision: the world showed you something true and you believed it. (¶34)
L076:             - 6.5 Ossification: the evidence moved and the anchor refused; not swinging is stubbornness in the costume of principle. (¶34)
L077:             - 6.6 Preference laundering: everyone is doing it now, so I guess it is fine. (¶34)
L078:                 - 6.6.1 Social proof gets laundered into belief that was never actually re-examined; the anchor moved, but on cost, not evidence. (¶34)
L079:             - 6.7 Anchor held: when cost changes but the anchor genuinely does not move, behavior still needs sorting. (¶34)
L080:                 - 6.7.1 Revealed preference: behavior snaps to the anchor once the cost of expressing it drops; the swing was suppression seen from outside. (¶34)
L081:                 - 6.7.2 Example: A charitable reading of the clothing story: not a shift in belief, just a cost that finally lifted. (¶34)
L082:                 - 6.7.3 Hysteresis: behavior fails to snap back even after the cost is gone, because the fear got installed deeply enough to run on its own. (¶34)
L083:                 - 6.7.4 Example: Still flinching at a threat that no longer exists. (¶34)
L084:             - 6.8 Nuance: Perspective gain: the evidence column does not require the reference itself to have moved, as when static tax law meets a growing grasp of it. (¶35)
L085:                 - 6.8.1 What was gained is a belief rather than a settled fact, so a second pass at the same unchanged reference can overturn it again. (¶35)
L086:             - 6.9 Rational revision and preference laundering can produce an identical new anchor from an identical old one: same destination, opposite process. (¶36)
L087:                 - 6.9.1 From outside they are indistinguishable, and often from inside too. (¶36)
L088:             - 6.10 The aphorism: no heart if you are not a socialist at 20, no head if you still are at 40. (¶37)
L089:                 - 6.10.1 It gets offered as an evidence-based claim: with age comes direct experience of taxes, hiring, saving, and incentives. (¶38)
L090:                 - 6.10.2 Structurally that is the lifecycle investing story: rational revision, tracking new information about how the world works. (¶38)
L091:             - 6.11 But the same trajectory falls out of a much less flattering process: aging brings assets, income, and things to lose from redistribution. (¶39)
L092:                 - 6.11.1 So views can drift to track self-interest rather than evidence: preference laundering with extra steps. (¶39)
L093:             - 6.12 Both processes produce the same trajectory and the same retrospective story, I used to be naive, so the shift itself tells you nothing. (¶39)
L094:         - 7 Theme: Real cases add three complications the toy model does not capture. (¶40)
L095:             - 7.1 Nuance: Confounded attribution: circumstances rarely change as pure evidence or pure cost; usually both move together. (¶41)
L096:                 - 7.1.1 A society opening up removes punishment and publicly stress-tests the reasons the old rule was defended on, which is genuine evidence. (¶41)
L097:                 - 7.1.2 A person can land on the objectively correct new anchor for the wrong reason, carried by cost while evidence happened to agree. (¶41)
L098:                 - 7.1.3 The flaw stays invisible until cost and evidence next point in opposite directions, when the process follows cost again and gets it wrong. (¶41)
L099:                 - 7.1.4 A correct belief reached by an unreliable process is not the same asset as a correct belief reached by a reliable one. (¶41)
L100:             - 7.2 Nuance: Misperceived reference: everything so far assumes the person correctly perceives where the reference sits, and they might not. (¶42)
L101:                 - 7.2.1 Example: They may think a norm still forbids something it no longer does, or that a market is still risk-off when it has turned. (¶42)
L102:                 - 7.2.2 This error is upstream of anchor, responsiveness, and policy entirely, corrupting every downstream judgment without any of the three being flawed. (¶42)
L103:             - 7.3 Nuance: Reference lag: what looks like a fixed anchor resisting a reference may be perfect tracking of a different, stale reference. (¶43)
L104:                 - 7.3.1 Example: Second-generation immigrant communities sometimes preserve older forms of a language than the one currently spoken in the country of origin. (¶43)
L105:                 - 7.3.2 Example: Quebec French keeps features that metropolitan French has dropped. (¶43)
L106:                 - 7.3.3 The real reference is the origin country's norm at the moment the family left, frozen the day they emigrated, while the living language kept moving. (¶43)
L107:                 - 7.3.4 The behavior looks like resistance to one reference and is actually perfect conformity to another that nobody ever intended to freeze. (¶43)
L108:                 - 7.3.5 Apparent principle, real conformity to the wrong clock. (¶43)
L109:         - 8 Nearly every pair above produces identical behavior and identical retrospective self-report. (¶44)
L110:             - 8.1 I have always believed this and I used to be naive are available to both the legitimate and illegitimate version, every time. (¶44)
L111:             - 8.2 That is what makes the problem hard, rather than merely a matter of introspecting harder. (¶44)
L112:             - 8.3 One thing separates the twins: what the person predicted, before the circumstance changed, that they would do once it did, and on what grounds. (¶45)
L113:             - 8.4 A prediction made under the old regime, with reasons attached, is much harder to fake retroactively than a story assembled after the fact. (¶45)
L114:             - 8.5 Example: I will understand incentives better by 40 is a falsifiable claim about future evidence, checkable against what actually changed in the person's reasoning. (¶46)
L115:             - 8.6 Example: I will have more to protect by 40 is a claim about future incentives wearing the vocabulary of insight. (¶46)
L116:             - 8.7 Example: I would wear this if society let me is a testable prediction about an anchor, not a post-hoc alibi for wherever behavior ended up. (¶46)
L117:             - 8.8 Where no such prior prediction exists, the case is underdetermined, and the default should not be the more flattering interpretation. (¶47)
L118:         - 9 Theme: Back at the mom's story: the word split into three, and the three came apart completely. (¶48)
L119:             - 9.1 The woman who swung with the revolution may have been running one unchanging rule; the investor who never changes may be the one failing. (¶48)
L120:             - 9.2 Consistency of behavior was never the thing the author admired or condemned; it was a proxy, and a bad one. (¶49)
L121:             - 9.3 What is actually evaluated is the process, not the consistency of behavior. (¶49)
L122:                 - 9.3.1 The process questions are what the anchor is, what the person is responsive to, and whether the movement came from evidence or cost. (¶49)
L123:             - 9.4 The process is mostly invisible, and the stories people tell about it afterward are mostly unfalsifiable. (¶49)
L124:             - 9.5 The only question the author really trusts is the one asked in advance. (¶49)
L125:                 - 9.5.1 Before anything changes: what do you think you will do when it does, and why? (¶49)
